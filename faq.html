Common questions

Short answers to what families ask most about V.O.I.C.E. (Variable Output for Individualized Communication and Expression). Tap a question to see its answer.

Getting it

Is V.O.I.C.E. really free?

Yes. There is no price, no ads and nothing to buy inside it. Gifts from people who want to help cover the cost of building and hosting it, but nothing is locked behind a gift. How gifts help

Do I need an account or an email?

No. You never sign up or log in. Open the link on the iPad and start. Open V.O.I.C.E. on the iPad

What devices does it work on?

V.O.I.C.E. is made and tested for the iPad, in Safari. It runs in a web browser, so it also opens on phones, Android tablets and computers. Some steps, like adding it to the Home Screen, work differently there.

Does it work without internet?

Yes. Once V.O.I.C.E. has opened one time, it works without the internet. To get the newest version, fully close it and open it again while the iPad is online. Add it to the Home Screen

Using it with my child

Will using a talker stop my child from learning to speak?

No. This is the worry families ask about most, and researchers have studied it. In those studies, children who used AAC did not lose speech, and many began to say more words. A talker gives your child a way to communicate now while speech keeps growing. The research, for therapists

Is my child “ready” for AAC?

There is nothing your child has to learn first. Children learn how a talker works by watching you use it and by trying it themselves, just as babies hear lots of talking before they talk. The best time to start is now. The first week

Why start with just six words?

Six words is a small start your child can learn. They are words a child needs every day: help, stop, eat, drink, bathroom and more. As your child uses them, more words appear in their own spots, and the first six never move. That way your child’s hand learns where each word lives. Watch the board grow

My child presses buttons over and over. Is that OK?

Yes. This is how children explore, the way babies babble before they talk. Each tap teaches what a button says. Answer it like talking when you can, and tap other words yourself as you talk. If the sound gets to be too much, turn the volume down instead of taking the iPad away.

Can siblings or other kids use the same iPad?

Yes. Several learners can share one iPad, each with their own board, settings and data. Brothers and sisters can also help by tapping words on your child’s board while they talk. That is called modeling, and it helps a lot. Guide: share one iPad

Privacy and safety

Where is my child’s information kept?

Only on the iPad. Words, settings, photos and teaching data are saved there and are not sent anywhere. There is no account, so no one else can see them, including me. Read the privacy page

What happens if the iPad breaks or gets reset?

Without a backup, a learner’s board and data can’t be brought back. Clearing Safari’s website data erases them too. Save a backup file now and then, to the Files app or iCloud Drive. On a new iPad, open V.O.I.C.E. and choose Restore from a backup file. How to save a backup

Help

Does this replace speech therapy?

No. V.O.I.C.E. supports, but doesn’t replace, an AAC assessment by a speech-language pathologist or team, and it is not speech therapy. If your child has a speech-language pathologist, show them V.O.I.C.E. They can help choose the board size and the next words.

I’m a therapist. Can I use it with clients?

Yes. Choose “Clinician or teacher” on the first screen to turn on teaching sessions and data. The clinician page explains the design and the teaching tools. For clinicians
