What V.O.I.C.E. can do

Every figure on this page is a small working piece of V.O.I.C.E. (Variable Output for Individualized Communication and Expression). Tap, hold and try things. Dibs, a Chicago pigeon, will show you around.

Growing

Start with six words, and grow without ever moving one.

Words keep their places

New words appear in spots saved for them from the start. Step through the stages of the 45-button main page.

Stage 1: 6 of 45 buttons. Dashed squares are saved spots.

Figure 2

The same path, every time

Because nothing moves, saying something becomes a movement the hand learns. Watch Dibs ride the L through “I want more,” with a ding-dong at every stop.

Speaking and typing

Grammar, little words, spelling and numbers, without leaving the board.

Figure 3

Fix the sentence with the Forms button

When the last word has other forms, the Forms button shows its whole family. Each sentence below has the wrong form. Tap Forms and pick the one that makes it right.

Figure 4

Hold a word for its dial

Press and hold a word, and its forms open around it. Past is always up, “-ing” right, “-s” down, and the last form left, so the hand learns one pattern for every word. Here, a tap opens it.

Figure 5

Eight little words, each with four more

A strip of eight little words never moves. Tap one and four related words open around it.

Figure 6

Say it with feeling

Press and hold the message bar and eight feelings open in a ring, each always in the same spot. Slide to one and let go to say the message once in that tone. Rest on it for a second and it becomes the learner’s mood, so every message sounds that way until it’s changed. A quick tap on the bar just says it.

The tones use your device’s own voice, so how much each one changes depends on that voice. Enhanced or Premium voices show it best, as in V.O.I.C.E. A tone changes the pitch, the speed and the ending punctuation (a “?” rises like a question, “…” trails off).

Also on the board: a keyboard for words that aren’t on the board yet, which can say each letter as it’s typed and offers word guesses in three fixed boxes; and a number pad that shows digits and says them as words (“24” is spoken “twenty-four”).

Scenes from your own camera

Turn a photo of a real place into words, without moving the board.

Figure 7

Make a scene from a photo

Aim the camera around the kitchen, then take a photo. V.O.I.C.E. finds things in it, suggests words that go with them, and makes a scene strip under the message bar. In the app, the photo and the finding happen on the iPad and never leave it.

Set it up for each learner

Colors, fonts, sizes and gaps change. The words never move.

Figure 8

Build a board for a learner

Start from one of V.O.I.C.E.’s ready-made settings, then change anything you like. Dibs sits on “more” the whole time, so you can see that no word ever moves. Tap words to hear them.

Shown at stage 3: 26 words are revealed and the other spots are saved for later. Labels shrink to fit their button, as they do in V.O.I.C.E.

Teaching

Opt-in teaching sessions, built on a prompt ladder with automatic fading.

Figure 9

Help that fades on its own

The model prompt is a ring around the target word, and an adult can bring Dibs along too. As the child succeeds, they fade in steps until the child finds the word alone. The adult gives the praise and the reward.

Figure 10

Backward chaining a sentence

For a sentence like “I want more,” V.O.I.C.E. can put the first words in the message bar so the child finishes it. Then the child does a little more each time.

Figure 11

Errorless sentence trials

While the prompt is at 50% or more, a wrong button does nothing, so the child practices only the right path. Blocked taps are still logged for the adult. Try tapping a wrong word.

Figure 12

When to move to the next stage

V.O.I.C.E. suggests the next stage when enough of the current stage’s words are proficient. By default that is 50%. A word counts when the child used it on their own on at least 3 days in the last 4 weeks, or mastered it in teaching. Tap the stage 2 words the child uses.

Adults and data

Tools for the grown-ups, kept on the iPad.

Figure 13

See where they tap: your heat map

This is the heat map from V.O.I.C.E.’s reports, filled in by you. Tap words on the board below. Taps on board words elsewhere on this page count too: the tiles in Figures 10 and 11, and any hidden words you find. Each one lands on that word’s spot.

Your taps stay in this page and disappear when you leave. In V.O.I.C.E., a folder button such as Food also counts the words used inside it.

Figure 14

A one-page summary, with things to try at home

The family page uses plain words and no percentages. It ends with words to practice at home: every word being taught right now, each with an everyday activity written for parents. A word that is mastered moves off the list. Pick a month and the words being taught.

Sample learner and sample numbers. In V.O.I.C.E., each word starts with the activity type that fits it, and the adult can edit the wording for any word.

Figure 15

Several learners, one iPad

Each learner has their own board, stage, look and data. Adult tools sit behind a 3-second hold and a passcode, so a child can’t open them by accident. Try holding the circle, then type any code with 1, 2 and 3. Codes starting with 1 show a V.O.I.C.E. word, 2 one of Dibs’s animal friends, and 3 a celebration.

Figure 16

A backup is one file you keep

Save a learner as a single file in the Files app. Open it on another iPad to bring everything back. Nothing goes through anyone’s server.

A backup file is not encrypted, so keep it somewhere safe.
