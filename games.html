Practice games

Four short games for parents and school teams. Practice the skills behind good teaching, with Dibs.

Find the favorite

About 3 minutes.

What you’ll practice:

Children change their minds, and choices take more than ten tries. This game shows the idea: offer two things, watch, write it down.

Why did that happen?

About 5 minutes.

What you’ll practice:

Behavior often has more than one reason. This game uses simple stories to practice spotting the clue.

Build a goal

About 4 minutes.

What you’ll practice:

A goal is written with the child’s team, starting from data about what the child does now.

Escape Lower Wacker

A few minutes per level.

What you’ll practice: Direction words: up, down, left, right, go and stop.

Dibs is lost under downtown Chicago, on the twisty streets of Lower Wacker Drive. Help him find the way out.

Play together. Say the word as you tap it: “Go! … Stop!” Let your child choose where Dibs goes, even the wrong way. Dead ends are part of the fun.

These games are for practice. They don’t assess a child. Only a full assessment by a qualified professional, such as a BCBA or school psychologist, can tell why a behavior happens or what a child’s goals should be.
