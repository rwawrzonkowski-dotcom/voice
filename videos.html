Start here

Three guides that get V.O.I.C.E. (Variable Output for Individualized Communication and Expression) on the iPad and talking.

Guide 1 for parents

Open V.O.I.C.E. and add it to the Home Screen

About 1 minute.

This puts V.O.I.C.E. on the iPad as its own app with its own icon.

  1. On the iPad, open Safari and go to the V.O.I.C.E. link. It has to be Safari for the next step to work.
  2. Tap the Share button (the square with an arrow pointing up).
  3. Tap Add to Home Screen, then tap Add.
  4. From now on, open V.O.I.C.E. from its icon. Once it has opened one time, it works without the internet.

Guide 2 for parents

Set up V.O.I.C.E. for your child

About 1 minute.

The first screen asks three things. You can change all of them later.

  1. Who you are. “Parent or caregiver” shows the everyday settings. “Clinician or teacher” adds teaching sessions and data.
  2. The learner’s name. This is optional.
  3. The board. Pick 24 buttons (large buttons for early skills, starts with 6 words), 45 buttons (the standard board; choose the option that starts with 6 words and reveals more as the learner grows) or 84 buttons (a larger board with smaller buttons).
  4. Choose a 4-digit passcode. It opens the adult menu. To get there, hold the small circle on the board for 3 seconds, then enter the passcode.

Not sure which board? Ask your child’s speech-language pathologist or behavior analyst.

Guide 3 for parents and staff

Model your first six words

About 1 minute.

V.O.I.C.E. starts with six words a child needs every day: help, stop, eat, drink, bathroom and more. The most helpful thing you can do is use the board yourself. This is called modeling.

  1. Tap the word as you say it. At snack: “Time to eat.” Pouring juice: “More?”
  2. Use everyday routines. Meals, the bathroom, play time and getting dressed all have a place for these words.
  3. Do not make the child copy you. Just show how it works, the way you would talk to a baby long before they talk back.
  4. Treat every tap as talking. If the child taps “more,” give more, even if it looks like an accident.
  5. Keep the iPad close. A voice only helps if it is within reach.

Guide 4 for parents and staff

Grow the board one stage at a time

About 1 minute.

Words keep their places as the board grows. New words appear in spots saved for them, so the words your child already knows never move.

  1. When your child is using the current words, open the adult menu (hold the small circle for 3 seconds, then enter your passcode).
  2. Look at Vocabulary stage to see progress. V.O.I.C.E. suggests the next stage when enough of the current words are in everyday use.
  3. You decide. An adult confirms every change.
  4. New words then appear in their own saved spots.

Want to see it happen? Step through the stages on the Features page. How V.O.I.C.E. decides is on the clinicians page.

Guide 5 for parents and staff

Change how the board looks

About 1 minute.

Colors, fonts, sizes and gaps can change to fit the learner. The words never move.

  1. Board looks to choose from: High contrast, CVI, Color-blind safe and Dim.
  2. Fonts to choose from: Atkinson Hyperlegible, Lexend and OpenDyslexic. You can also pick larger labels and wider gaps.
  3. Change one thing at a time and watch whether your child finds the words more easily.
  4. If you are not sure which look fits, ask your child’s therapist or teacher.

Try the looks on the home page board, or build a board in Figure 8 on the Features page.

Guide 6 for parents and staff

Make a scene from a photo

About 1 minute.

An adult can turn a photo of a place, like the kitchen, into a strip of words. The main board does not move.

  1. Take a photo of the place.
  2. V.O.I.C.E. finds things in the photo and suggests words that go with them. It only suggests things it is at least 40% sure of.
  3. Untick anything you do not want. If nothing was found, you can draw a box around each thing by hand instead.
  4. Name the scene, then choose to use it. Nothing is saved until you choose to.
  5. The scene shows as a separate strip, so the core words stay where they are.

In V.O.I.C.E., the photo and the finding happen on the iPad and never leave it. The Features page shows a practice version with a drawing.

Guide 7 for parents and staff

Say it with feeling

About 1 minute.

V.O.I.C.E. can say a message in a feeling: happy, excited, silly, question, calm, sad, mad or scared. Each feeling always sits in the same place on a ring.

  1. Press and hold the message bar for half a second. The ring of eight feelings opens, with “Normal” in the center.
  2. Slide to a feeling and let go to say the message once in that tone.
  3. Rest on a feeling for one second and it becomes the learner’s mood, so every message sounds that way until it is changed.
  4. A quick tap on the message bar just says it.

A feeling changes the pitch, the speed and the ending punctuation. It uses the iPad’s own voice, so Enhanced or Premium voices show it best.

Guide 8 for parents and staff

Help that fades

About 1 minute.

Teaching sessions are optional. When an adult uses them, V.O.I.C.E. can show a model prompt and then take it away slowly.

  1. A ring appears around the word to tap. An adult can add Dibs the pigeon, or one of his Chicago friends, too.
  2. As the child succeeds, the help fades in steps until the child finds the word alone.
  3. The adult gives the praise and the reward. The app does not.
  4. For a sentence like “I want more,” V.O.I.C.E. can put the first words in the message bar so the child finishes it, then the child does a little more each time.

The details, such as prompt ladders, errorless trials and chaining, are on the clinicians page.

Guide 9 for parents

Read the parent summary

About 1 minute.

V.O.I.C.E. can write a one-page summary of how your child is talking. It uses plain words and no percentages, and it prints cleanly.

  1. Pick a month.
  2. Read what changed: the new words your child started using, their favorite words, and the longest message.
  3. Look at Words to practice at home. Every word being taught right now is there, each with an everyday activity written for parents.
  4. When a word is mastered, it moves off the list.

Everything stays on the iPad unless an adult exports it. See Privacy.

Keep it safe

Shared iPads and backups.

Guide 10 for parents and staff

Share one iPad between learners

About 1 minute.

Several learners can share one iPad. Each has their own board, stage, look and data.

  1. Adult tools sit behind a 3-second hold and a passcode, so a child cannot open them by accident.
  2. Hold the small circle on the board for 3 seconds, then enter the passcode.
  3. Keep the passcode private, so only adults can open the adult tools.

Try the hold and passcode game in Figure 15 on the Features page.

Guide 11 for everyone

Save a backup, and bring it back

About 1 minute.

Everything V.O.I.C.E. knows about a learner lives only on the iPad. A backup is the one safety net. It is a single file you keep.

  1. Hold the adult circle for 3 seconds and enter your passcode.
  2. Open Backup.
  3. Save the file somewhere safe, like the Files app or iCloud Drive.
  4. To bring a learner back, or move to a new iPad, open V.O.I.C.E. and choose Restore from a backup file.

Clearing Safari’s website data erases V.O.I.C.E.’s learners, settings and photos, so save a new backup now and then, and after any big change. A backup file is not encrypted, so keep it somewhere safe.
